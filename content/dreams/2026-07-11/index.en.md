Today I dreamed I came out of a museum and got a ride in K’s Tesla. But it turned out that his black Tesla had a handcart stuck to its back with magnets. When the Tesla sped up, the magnets pulled the handcart forward. As the handcart’s driver, I had to keep steering to get the strongest pull. The road down the mountain was very steep, and there were close calls all the way. At an open staircase full of gaps, I had to disconnect. The gaps were too big. The handcart would have fallen through them into a bottomless drop, while the Tesla was too big to fall through. After that I left the cart behind and walked. The place had green hills and clear water, and it was crowded with visitors, a bit like Huangshan. In a hollow not far away stood a pavilion built of mossy stone, partly set into the mountain. Then it hit me: I’ve been here before. Isn’t this Luzhou? Haha. I suddenly felt like visiting the old place again, and I walked toward the pavilion. Oddly, you couldn’t get in through the front door. Instead there was an entrance in the mountainside next to it. They must have dug a tunnel through the mountain to reach it. When I got closer, I saw four big black-and-white letters above the entrance: MBPC! Where have I ended up? I walked in, puzzled, and inside the opening the space suddenly widened into a canteen hall. This canteen… also felt familiar. The 50-yuan premium dishes window seemed to have the same dishes as the last time we went (to a highway service area). One of them, a steamed sea bass, didn’t look very fresh. Then I needed the toilet and started looking for one. The signs said to take the elevator down, so I took the elevator. In the elevator, a girl with dark skin and a bold, confident air gave me a surprised look. She looked me up and down and asked, unsure: “Hui Yuchen?” ???? My brain seemed to explode. Since when did I know a foreign girl like this, and one who speaks Chinese too? Her parents and her little brother looked at each other, with no idea what was going on. The girl went on: “Don’t you remember? In primary school we danced the xxx dance from Swan Lake together in the xxxx castle here.” It was as if a lot of memories came back all at once. Some blurry pink backgrounds slowly came up in my mind. It was true. She kept remembering. Back then she went to another primary school, not mine. A xxx organization had taken pupils from both schools on an outing together, and that is how we met. I felt a bit embarrassed, since I had no memory of it at all. It felt like I owed something to the person who still remembered. We kept talking as we walked out of the elevator. By now I had forgotten about the toilet. I followed the girl’s family out of the elevator and out of the cave through another exit. We were now on the other side of the pavilion. The pavilion was strange too. The mountain wrapped it up tightly, a bit like a spring bamboo shoot breaking through the soil. We kept walking and talking along the mountain path on that side. The girl took out Instagram. After we added each other, she opened that organization’s Instagram home page, and you could still find photos of the outing from more than ten years ago! I opened my eyes wide and tried to make out what we looked like as kids in those blurry photos, but I could never see them clearly. Dream resolution is just too low. After a while, the girl’s white dad said something to her in a language I didn’t understand. She turned to me and said her family had something to do and had to leave first. I didn’t want to say goodbye, and I was very excited about this strange meeting, but we had to part. After we went our separate ways, I walked up a road into the mountains. Google Maps said this was a small town north of Montreal. After a long walk, as I climbed higher, snow started to appear on the road. On my sidewalk, the snow was half a meter deep. The early-summer weather had already warmed up, so the snow wasn’t hard like in deep winter. Every step was slippery and wet and squeezed out lots of muddy water. I carefully made my way forward in the footprints in the snow. On the left, a steep side road branched off and went straight up to the summit. The snow on both sides of this small asphalt road was even messier than on mine. Lots of cars were stuck in it at odd angles, like pieces of chocolate in the cream of a cake that a bunch of kids had dug through. Along both sides of the road stood rows of fancy, rich-looking townhouses. It felt a bit like the rich neighborhood on the slope of Mount Royal in Outremont. I laughed to myself: rich neighborhood, so what? They still have to dig their cars out of the snow. But the houses still made me curious. Dragging my heavy feet, I started to cross the road. When I reached the bike lane, a few fully geared-up cyclists nearly ran into me, and a bus nearly squeezed them…
