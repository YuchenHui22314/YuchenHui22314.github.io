In the later part of the dream, a cold wave swept in, and heavy snow came down from the sky. I hurried to the dock below my building to photograph the river ice. There was a small pool of still water at the dock, and it had frozen over. I crouched on the ice, held the camera as close to the surface as I could, and looked for an angle to shoot the downtown towers across from the dock. The sun was setting, and its orange light looked down gently on the snowy world. It gave a calm, contented feeling. The low sun was playful too. As I turned the camera, she played hide-and-seek with me among the towers, and I could never find the right angle to catch her most perfect face. After a while, I came to the edge of the big river. By now 80% of its surface was frozen solid. I walked on the river for a while, really excited. Then I opened my phone and saw a message from XJH. He said today’s weather forecast was wrong. He also sent a GIF that showed how this cold wave had played out. Amazingly, the snow had been caused by a typhoon that made landfall on the Korean Peninsula. In the GIF, the typhoon icon came ashore on the peninsula’s south coast, and the white snow area quickly followed its path, covered the whole peninsula, and reached Tieling on the China–North Korea border, which was our city (in reality I’m not in Tieling, and Tieling isn’t on the China–North Korea border). But the white had a hole in it at Tieling. This was absurd: it was snowing right here. I started thinking about the cause of this bug. Maybe the staff hadn’t cut out the background layer of the typhoon icon, so the background layer covered the white snow layer. That’s why, when the typhoon reached Tieling, there was nothing at all around it.

Later, I dreamed that spring came: the dock thawed, the big river flowed again, and much of the ice on the Dongda sports field melted.
