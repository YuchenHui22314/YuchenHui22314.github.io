Scene 4: at home. The living room looked much longer than in real life, and completely empty. In the corners lay leftover sawdust and bits of concrete from renovation work, as if an earthquake had just hit. At the far end was a window, separated from the living room by a trapdoor. Behind the trapdoor was a small dining room, rather cramped, with wobbly tables and chairs scattered around. Outside, rain was pouring down, so only a little grayish-white light came in through the window, which made the room feel even more oppressive. In one corner of the room was the entrance to a water room. This water room was actually the hub that controlled the building’s drainage system. Its floor was open, and below it was a big funnel that collected rainwater from the roof. Right now the water in it was rushing and formed a big, roaring whirlpool. The water poured out of some iron drainpipes nearby; they must have been collecting rain from the whole building. On the wall in the middle of the room was a lever that seemed to control the pace of the water. To reach the lever, you had to cross the open floor like a rope bridge: a few multicolored beams were wedged between two walls like the planks of a rope bridge, which never felt safe. The beams formed a kind of staircase, but there were big gaps between the steps, and one careless move would drop you into the whirlpool in the funnel below. Luckily, on the wall next to each beam there was a short piece of hemp rope you could hold on to for extra safety. So I nervously went back and forth across the beams to control the water, with my heart in my throat. Then, one time, just as I lightly grabbed a rope on the wall to move between beams, the rope came off the wall! My heart started racing. I thought, good thing I gave it a tug before setting off; otherwise, if I had pulled on it while crossing and found nothing to hold, I might have been lost for good. All that was left in the wall now was a tiny bit of rope core stuck in it, like a drawing of magnetic field lines coming out of a plane. I had no choice but to lower my center of gravity and keep moving carefully…
