I woke up suddenly in the middle of the night, gripped by a strange sense of danger. The scene had already shifted to home, and I was sleeping on the sofa. Out of instinct I first looked at the backpack beside me: still there. Then my hand found the money under the blanket, and I could breathe a little easier. Just as I got up to go to the bathroom, in the faint light coming through the living room window, a dark shadow on the white curtain made me stop breathing. When I pulled the curtain open, I saw a man hanging from a rope against the wall, working with tools to remove the iron bars outside our kitchen window. He saw me too and gave me an evil grin. I grabbed my phone on instinct, about to shout that I was calling the police, but before I could, he drew the numbers “110” in the air with his finger. For some reason I didn’t call the police; instead I tried to lock the kitchen door. Ridiculously, only the bottom half of the kitchen door was left! Just as I picked up my phone to make a call, the robber had already landed in front of me, as if he had dropped from the sky. He was a neat man in gold-rimmed glasses, with the clever face of a science whiz. I was about to say something when he instantly raised a silver pistol, and I dropped my phone on instinct and gave the French salute (hands up). He smiled slyly and said, “All right, hand over the money,” making a give-me gesture with his free hand. I didn’t dare hesitate and gave him the 1,500 US dollars hidden under my blanket. “This is 1,500 dollars, I really don’t have any other money.” “Stop the act. Take me to where your family keeps the money.” “I haven’t been home in a long time. I don’t remember where we used to keep money.” I thought, I really don’t know where the money is… though there’s still some money in my backpack… I hope he doesn’t find it… He lowered the pistol and strolled toward the bedroom, saying, “Fine, let’s start with the bedroom.” The instant he turned around, my mind raced and worked it out: I can run! So I dashed for the door; the cabinet blocked his line of fire. I made it into the elevator lobby and hesitated for a second about knocking on the neighbors’ door, but gave up at once: no time. I ran straight from the elevator lobby into the stairwell and started down, using the escape plan I had rehearsed in my head countless times. One floor down, I hesitated about calling for help, but I ran down one more floor before I started pounding on doors like crazy for help. Then I woke up. My heart was racing, and my body felt so hot…
