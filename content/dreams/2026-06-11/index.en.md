Today, in the first dream, I played a match against Taufik Hidayat. I gave every point my full focus. In the third game, the player across the net suddenly turned into Z, and Taufik coached both of us from the side of the court. I stayed two or three points ahead up to 13:10. Then on this point, Z answered my clear with a jump smash from behind the baseline, with real Taufik style, and closed in to 11:13. I felt a chill, but I kept playing carefully. Maybe I was so focused that I woke up too many senses. Suddenly my sense of touch from real life started coming back, and I realized the dream was about to end. I was really annoyed, because I wanted so much to finish this game…

In the second dream, my school held an event at the venue of the aces championnat, a badminton tournament. They had made lots of good food, like a canteen. After a big meal, I rode my bike home. On the way I passed Kunlavut’s home and dropped in for a visit. Kunlavut’s mom was there too and welcomed me warmly. In the dream, Kunlavut and I were good friends, and we hung out together for a long time. After Kunlavut saw me off, he posted an Instagram story of us hanging out and tagged me. I went downstairs and took a slow walk around Kunlavut’s neighborhood. It was full of green trees and really lovely. The only problem was that the dark clouds in the distance looked like rain, so I walked faster. Just as I got to where I had parked and was about to ride off, I found a few thieves taking my bike apart! They had already cut the spokes, and the wheels were bent. They had also pried open the bike’s blue frame, showing the complicated wiring inside (even though mine isn’t an e-bike…). I angrily went up and told them to pay for it. They said very calmly that this was impossible. We will be taking these bikes away. It turned out they were a well-known bike theft gang in Montreal, a big organized operation. Looking around, I saw about 100 people in small groups on this square, taking apart every bike parked there. There were too many of them, and I couldn’t do anything about it. A few police officers next to them were chatting and laughing with them. Clearly someone was protecting them. Now I started to regret it. If I had known, I would have ridden my 50-dollar bike, not this 800-dollar one… Then I watched them load my bike onto a truck and drive off.
