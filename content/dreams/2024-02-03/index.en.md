MK lived in a library not far from our home. The library’s entrance was inside a large building. The entrance was decorated in a very modern way: pure white floor and ceiling, with clear glass walls. When MK went in, he would take a bunch of keys out of his pocket to open the door. He had shown me around a few times. That day he wasn’t there, and I quietly slipped in behind someone else. Inside it was quite big, and everything was done in solid colors, like Polytechnique: on each floor, the floor, walls and ceiling were all one color (orange, for example). I met the librarian and, after asking, learned that this wasn’t MK’s home but his office. The librarian said that since I was a UdeM student, they could give me a key too, and I happily accepted. The library had two floors. On earlier visits we had only stayed on the first floor; this was the first time I learned there was a second one. So I went up the stairs (a bit like the big staircase at Mila, but much smaller) and found an event going on in a classroom. I forget exactly what it was; it seemed to be a dice game like Monopoly. I wasn’t interested, so I left. The library also had an exit leading outside. Someone else (I forget who) and I went out to explore. Outside was a small snowy mountain. The cliffs were steep, and the snow and ice slipped under our feet. I followed the person ahead of me up the slope, step by step. He climbed fast, so I could barely keep up, and I felt shaky and scared. Before the summit, we had to climb an ice (or snow) cliff at about 60 degrees, with only a handful of footholds. I steeled myself and followed him up. Luckily nothing went wrong. We finally reached the top! On the other side of the ridge, the slope was much gentler, and skiers were racing across the snowfield. My eyes followed a skier crossing the ridge from the other side, back toward the way we had come, and I gasped: how dangerous the path behind us was! I felt nervous for that skier. Looking into the distance, we saw that the horizon was a hazy white, wrapped in fog. Only a white plain at the foot of the mountain was clearly visible. The white plain seemed to be split into pieces, like pure white marble tiles with a few cracks. We started down the other side of the ridge to take a closer look. But halfway down, the weather suddenly changed, and the white plain actually melted! The pieces turned out to be frozen rice paddies, and once they melted they became calm, black water. Now the plain looked like black marble tiles.
