Later, I was at a badminton tournament, checking into a hotel. The hotel floor was a long, dim, retro-style corridor (like the corridor at the entrance of the Cold War museum, but only as high as one person). On both sides of the corridor were guest rooms. Each door had a plaque with the names of the athletes staying in that room. I ran into Li Shifeng in the corridor; he was coming the other way, also looking for his room. We chatted briefly and went on looking for our own rooms. I remembered my room’s plaque as a red background with “Yuchen HUI” in white, so I searched by that pattern, but I got to the end of the corridor without finding it. So I dropped that assumption and had to start looking again. On the way I also saw Weng Hongyang’s name, and I couldn’t help wondering who my roommate would be. Finally, on a small door that looked much lower and older than the others, I saw a plaque with my name. It said my roommate was a foreigner; his name was all in Latin letters, and I couldn’t read it. The plaque was dark red and a little rusty, and our names seemed to be printed in an off-white paint. Looking at this door, for some reason I thought of Sherlock Holmes’s front door at 221 Baker Street in 19th-century London. (Too much Detective Conan, probably.) When I pushed the door open, I was relieved. From the door I had expected a dirty, messy slum, but inside it looked nicely done, with a big floor-to-ceiling window, and sunlight was coming in. My roommate had dark yellowish skin and black hair; I thought he was probably Indian. We chatted a bit in English (or French? I don’t remember). He said we were lucky: this room had no air conditioning at first, so the organizers had taken an air conditioner from somewhere else and installed it for us. I was quite tired and wanted to shower and sleep, but he kept talking. Then he suddenly said he had read my paper, and that the related work section was very well written, not like someone publishing a paper on their own for the first time. (So this wasn’t a badminton tournament but a paper competition?) I thought, how do you compete with papers? Whoever writes better? There’s no head-to-head… or is it a knockout… how would that even work… Anyway, I was very confused. Then I dreamed that to get from our room to the corridor, we suddenly had to cross a rope bridge. A layer of green court mat or something was stuck on the bridge, and the two of us lay on the bridge and started peeling it off. Later the organizers brought in a flying machine to remove it, which made the bridge sway. I lay flat on it to keep my center of gravity low; otherwise I would have fallen off. Then I woke up.
