## Ingredients

### A. Base veggies (stir-fried until half-cooked, then laid in the bottom of the bowl)

* Bean sprouts
* Lettuce (or another crisp leafy green like A-choy)
* Salt (for the base veggies)
* Dried chilies / chili pieces (optional; tossing a few in while you stir-fry the base veggies works too)

### B. Meat slices and marinade

* Any meat, thinly sliced: pork tenderloin or pork collar, beef, chicken breast, fish fillets all work (thin slices cook faster)
* Shaoxing cooking wine
* Light soy sauce (a little)
* Ground pepper (optional)
* Salt (a little, or none at all; the broth base is already salty)
* Five-spice powder (my upgrade for extra aroma)
* Starch (the key: it locks in moisture and keeps the meat tender and silky)
* Egg (add it or skip it; in my tests it didn’t make much difference)

### C. Broth and seasonings

* Beef-tallow hot-pot base (the small blocks from the supermarket: 1 block per batch)
* Water or chicken stock (chicken stock tastes richer)
* Pixian chili bean paste (**a must**—it’s the “soul” of the dish)
* Sugar (optional, to balance the heat and the salt)
* Oyster sauce (optional)
* Light soy sauce (usually you can skip it or add just a little, since the bean paste + hot-pot base are already salty)

### D. Optional add-ins (anything goes, toss in whatever you like)

* Bamboo shoots, kelp, wood ear mushrooms, tofu, enoki mushrooms, and other hot-pot favorites

### E. Sizzling oil and toppings

* Chopped scallions, minced garlic
* Cooking oil (for the sizzling pour)

---

## Steps

### 1) Stir-fry the base veggies until half-cooked and line the bowl

1. Heat a little oil in the wok, then add the bean sprouts and lettuce (plus some chili flakes or dried chili pieces).
2. Add **just enough salt** (only as much as these two vegetables need).
3. Stir-fry over high heat until **about 50% cooked** (no longer raw, but still crunchy), then spread them in the bottom of a big bowl and set aside.

---

### 2) Make the broth right in the same wok (no need to wash it)

1. Put **1 block of beef-tallow hot-pot base** in the wok and let it melt over low heat.
2. Add water/chicken stock and bring to a boil.
3. Add the **chili bean paste** (the key step I keep stressing).
4. Taste before deciding whether to add sugar/oyster sauce/a little light soy sauce (it’s usually salty enough already, so don’t go dumping things in right off the bat).

---

### 3) Marinate the meat (get this done before you start cooking)

1. Slice the meat thin.
2. Add cooking wine, light soy sauce (a little), pepper/salt (a little or none), and **five-spice powder**.
3. Add the starch and massage it in well (egg optional).
4. Let it rest for a few minutes so the coating can “set.”

> Bottom line: **starch is really important**—without a starch coating, the meat turns dry and tough more easily and leaks its juices.

---

### 4) Add the meat and add-ins, cook through

1. Once the broth is boiling, start with the add-ins that can take a longer simmer (bamboo shoots, kelp, wood ear, tofu, etc.).
2. Add the meat and stir to separate the slices; once it changes color, simmer briefly until cooked through. Or put the meat in first and the vegetables after, either way works; call it “concrete analysis of concrete conditions.”
3. When everything is cooked, pour **the whole wok**—broth, meat, add-ins and all—over the half-cooked base veggies waiting in the bowl.

---

### 5) Finish with sizzling oil (for the aroma, and for the sense of ceremony)

1. Scatter chopped scallions and minced garlic over the top.
2. In a separate small pan (or a clean small ladle), heat the oil until it’s good and hot.
3. Pour it straight over the scallions and garlic; when you hear that loud “tsssss” and the garlic aroma hits you, you’re done.
